## Consolidation decisions

The public front door is organized around questions: can a graph improve reconciliation, can the evaluator distinguish evidence from assertions, and does a forecast improve a simple baseline? Ontology, dated joins, calculation tools and review procedures support those questions.

Version numbers belong in provenance and study history rather than becoming separate headline projects. Failed reviews and attempts remain part of the evidence. The history page separates changed tasks, changed assistance and repeated sessions instead of pooling them into one success trend.

No source directories were deleted, renamed or moved. Existing tools may depend on their paths, and failed attempts are research records. This publication reduces duplication in the presentation without silently erasing the development history.

## What earns another report

A candidate should have one bounded question, identifiable primary evidence, a useful baseline, a reproducible decisive calculation or audit, and a clear statement of what could change the conclusion. Its publication basis must be established before exporting source material or empirical results. A report assembled from licensed inputs is not cleared merely because it contains only aggregates.

Prioritize a candidate that adds a different, explainable result. A later version number, larger architecture or larger test count is not enough. Follow the [publication procedure](PUBLICATION.md) and [research-review skill](../skills/review-research-result/SKILL.md) one report at a time.

This is a curated public edition of the broader workspace. The remaining archive is an explicit future review queue, not an implicit claim that every private artifact belongs on GitHub.
